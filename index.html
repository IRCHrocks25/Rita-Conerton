The Method

A zipper has two sides, and it only works when both move forward together.

The Zipper Change Approach.

Two sides. One successful adoption.

One side is the technology: the platform, the data, the integrations, the governance, the deployment. The other side is the people: leadership, trust, role clarity, confidence, and the behavior that has to change. Pull one side ahead of the other and nothing closes.

How the Zipper Approach works

Phase 01Assess
I find what will help or block adoption. Sponsor alignment, employee fear, role and workflow impact, capability gaps, and the champions and strengths the organization already has. Findings become a prioritized roadmap rather than a diagnosis.
Phase 02Activate
The roadmap becomes a sequenced rollout plan with owners and timing, written so your delivery team can run it: leadership alignment, manager enablement, targeted communication, role-based enablement, champion activation and resistance response.
Phase 03Sustain
Adoption is designed to outlast the launch date. Reinforcement, measurement and course correction are planned in from the start rather than improvised after go-live.

Two connected sides. One successful adoption.

Why This Exists

Technology and people have to move forward together.

I spent fifteen years watching organizations invest heavily in a system and treat the people expected to use it as a communications task at the end of the plan. The pattern never changed. The technology worked. The training happened. And the morning after go-live, people went back to what they knew.

That gap is not a training problem and it is not a communications problem. It is an adoption problem, and it can be designed for. AI has only made it more urgent, because AI changes how people make decisions, how they work, and what they believe their role is worth.

A solution can work exactly as designed and still fail to deliver value.

The real test of transformation is what people do differently after the project team leaves.

Case Study

Global manufacturer, SAP rollout across three facilities

A global manufacturer moved off legacy systems onto SAP across three manufacturing facilities, touching more than 200 business functions and roughly 3,000 users. Leadership understood early that technical deployment alone would not decide the outcome.

The work

+

Stakeholder analysis and change impact assessment across all three sites.

+

Organizational readiness assessments and sponsorship alignment.

+

A communications and champion structure that gave the rollout local voices.

+

Role-based training, hands-on workshops, process guides, job aids and nine learning videos.

+

A phased go-live with structured hypercare support at each facility.

All three facilities went live. More than 200 business functions were carried into the new environment, a virtual learning portal was left behind for ongoing knowledge transfer, and positive employee attitudes toward SAP adoption improved by 10.5 percent across the implementation, measured through workforce readiness and adoption surveys.

Technology transformations succeed when people, process and technology are addressed together.
